Our Story

Ensō no Sato

Japanese dining has a way of making even the smallest details matter. The changing of the seasons, the temperature of the rice, the way a piece of fish is cut, or the moment a dish is placed before you. Things that may seem simple, but take years to truly understand.

It is this appreciation for the craft, and for the experience of sharing a meal, that inspired Ensō no Sato.

Set in the heart of Huntington Village, we wanted to create a place that captures two sides of Japanese dining: the quiet intimacy of omakase and the lively, unhurried spirit of an izakaya.

At our omakase counter, the evening unfolds at its own pace. The menu changes with the seasons, guided by the seafood available and the chef’s understanding of how best to prepare it. Some ingredients call for a little creativity. Others are best left almost untouched. Knowing the difference is part of the craft.

Beyond the counter, the mood is more relaxed. Sushi and sashimi, dishes from the binchotan grill, a good bottle of sake, or a cocktail shared over conversation. A place to discover something new, order another favorite, and stay a little longer than planned.

To us, Japanese hospitality is not about formality. It’s about being attentive without being intrusive, taking pride in the smallest details, and making people feel genuinely welcome.

There is a time for an intimate dinner at the counter, and a time for gathering around a table with friends. Both deserve the same care.

And perhaps that’s what we love most about a good restaurant. It becomes part of people’s lives — a place for celebrations, familiar faces, spontaneous evenings, and all the moments in between.

That’s the kind of place we hope Ensō no Sato will always be.
